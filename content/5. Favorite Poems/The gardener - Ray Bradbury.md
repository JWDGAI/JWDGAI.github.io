Everyone must leave something behind when he dies.
A child, a book,  a house, a painting,
A wall built, a pair of shoes made, a garden planted. 
Something your hand touched some way
so your soul has somewhere to go when you die, 
and when people look at that tree or that flower you remain.
It doesn't matter what you do, so long as you change something from the way it was into something that's like you after you take your hands away.
The difference between the man who just cuts lawns and a real gardener is in the touching. 
The lawn-cutter might just as well not have been there at all; the gardener will be there a lifetime.
				
				Adapted from Fahrenheit 451
